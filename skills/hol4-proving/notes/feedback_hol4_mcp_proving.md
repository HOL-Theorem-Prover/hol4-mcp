## Syntax and structural forms
- `>~`/`>-`/`>>` same precedence, left-associative.
- `*`/`DIV`/`MOD` same precedence, left-associative: `a * b MOD c` = `(a * b) MOD c`. Symptom of a mis-parenthesised quotation: a "trivially true" arithmetic `by simp[]` fails, or a qmatch raises. Fix: explicit parens matching the goal's actual term (verify via `aconv`, not the display).
- Resume header labels are UNQUOTED: `Resume thm[Label]:` — writing `Resume thm["Label"]:` parse-errors at replay and cascades into misleading "No such label" extraction failures (the `suspend "Label"` tactic call itself DOES take a quoted string).
- Qualify overloaded constructors in `>~` patterns: `Module$Constructor`.
- Suspend/Resume/Finalise structural facts: body in `Resume thm[Name]: <tactics> QED`; dispatcher `suspend` labels must match Resume labels exactly. See [[feedback_suspend_resume]] for dispatch and inlining craft.

## Which normaliser — what each one does to your assumptions
- **`simp` uses assumptions AS THEY STAND; `fs`/`gvs`/`rw` SIMPLIFY the assumptions first.** (`simp` *is* `asm_simp_tac` — bossLib.sml — so "simp ignores assumptions" is false.) Two consequences, both measured: an assumption that must be simplified before it is a usable rewrite (`(if T then x else y) = 2` vs goal `x = 2`) closes under `fs` and leaves `simp` unchanged; and a DISJUNCTIVE assumption (`P \/ Q`) is split into one goal per disjunct by `fs`/`gvs`/`rw`, not by `simp` — so a `>-` after one of them can dispatch a branch you did not mean. A negated conjunction is NOT split: `¬(a ∧ b)` normalises to `a ⇒ ¬b`, one goal.
- `simp[]` rewrites the goal only.
- `fs[…]` rewrites both goal + asms; retains the equation. Auto-closes a goal that becomes literally an assumption (or simplifies to T via one). **Therefore: after `fs[]`/`gvs[]`/`rfs[]`, never add `first_assum ACCEPT_TAC` or `metis_tac []` on a goal that's already a hyp — `fs` closed it. Read the state.**
- `gvs[…]` rewrites both AND drops asms it used. Aggressive — can consume the asm a downstream tactic needed; swap to `fs`/`simp`.
- **`gvs[L]` chains through asm-equalities; `fs[L]` doesn't.** When rewriting an asm via `L` produces an intermediate that another asm-equality would simplify further, `fs` stops, `gvs` continues.
- **`gvs[def]` is strictly stronger than `fs[def] >> gvs[]`** for record-projection / state-equality unfolding. The combined form lets gvs use the unfolded field equalities AS substitutions inside the same pass; splitting them leaves the unfolded equalities as inert asms that the followup `gvs[]` doesn't re-rewrite. When goal-count after `fs[def] >> gvs[]` looks suspiciously high (e.g. vacuous `F` or `outcome = outcome'` goals that should have closed), try `gvs[def]` first.
- **`gs[def]` fires conditional-implication asms (`∀x. P x ⇒ Q x`) as rewrites** when antecedents are satisfied by other asms. Reach for `gs` when the goal differs from an asm only by a name that some IH equates.
- `fs[]` HO-instantiates outer `∃` from free-skolem asms; `simp[]` does not. Picking a witness → prefer `simp[]`; switch to `fs[]` after `qexists_tac`.
- **`simp` may simplify an assumption FURTHER than the immediate rewrite** — its side-solver can fuse the result with other asms (e.g. collapsing two equalities into a derived one). Re-read the goal before writing tactics that assume the un-fused form.
- One-asm surgical rewrite: `qpat_x_assum \`…\` (mp_tac o REWRITE_RULE [def])`.

## Rewriting that loops, oscillates, or blows up
Symptom is usually a `hol_state_at`/`hol_check_proof` TIMEOUT. That is YOUR looping tactic, not a slow prefix — diagnose by cheating the frontier before the tactic ([[feedback_replay_discipline]] §TIMEOUT), never by widening `timeout=`.

- ⛔ **Unfolding a RECURSIVE definition (or a recursive `_sem`/predicate) with `simp`/`fs`/`gvs`/`rw[<def>]` loops** — it rewrites forever, worst under `Induct` (rewrites the def inside its own IH). ALWAYS `simp[Once <def>]`, or extract/peel the recursive clause with `>~`. Holds for BOTH a recursive encoder def and a recursive semantics predicate unfolded in the same proof (unfold each with `Once`).
- **`GSYM` rewriting can loop.** `rewrite_tac [GSYM thm]` on equation `lhs = rhs` rewrites `rhs → lhs`; if `lhs` again matches in the new term the rewriter re-applies endlessly. Use `simp [Once (GSYM thm)]` or `once_rewrite_tac [GSYM thm]` for a single pass.
- **Both `a = b` and `b = a` (symmetric equalities) in the assumptions make `rw`/`fs` oscillate → hang.** Keep ONE orientation — `kall_tac` the redundant direction (e.g. when a tactic introduces the reverse of an equality already in context).
- **bossLib `simp`/`rw`/`fs`/`gvs` AC-canonicalize nat/int sums (ARITH_ss), so operand order is not yours to pick**: instantiation can leave a lemma's sum in non-canonical order relative to the goal, and `simp [Once ADD_COMM]` cannot fix it — the reorientation fires but the same call's normalizer flips it back (net no-op). Bridge with `once_rewrite_tac [ADD_COMM]` (plain rewrite, no normalizer) immediately before the matching step, no simp in between. Restating the lemma in the goal's order doesn't work either — the lemma's own proof re-canonicalizes.
- `fs[big_def] >> metis_tac[]` and `gvs[big_def]` time out — keep the def folded; use `simp[Once <def>]`; or split conjuncts.
- **Extract one clause to control unfolding.** When `simp[Once big_def]` picks the wrong clause (e.g., unfolds `Loop` when you wanted `Tick`), pull out the target clause as a local theorem and `simp[that_clause]` instead. Avoids the multi-clause whole-def blowup and the "which clause does Once pick" guesswork.
- **Unfold to compute, RE-FOLD to apply the IH.** An induction step that unfolds a definition to expose the structural step leaves the goal unfolded while the IH stays folded — nothing matches. `gvs[GSYM <def>]` re-folds the goal AND fires the now-matching IH in the same pass; cheaper than unfolding the IH to chase the goal.
- **Never `mp_tac` a conditional equation that binds a variable to a large nested term, then `simp`/`fs` with distributive/splitting lemmas** (`option_le_max(_right)`, `option_map2_max_add`, AC, …). `simp` substitutes the term, then the split/distribute rules expand it combinatorially → nontermination / memory blowup. Resolve the variable with a targeted terminating step (case-split on the guard, antisymmetry), kept OUT of any broad `simp`/`fs`.
- **`MEM_ZIP` / `EL_MAP` side-conditions don't auto-discharge.** `simp [MEM_ZIP]` won't fire without an explicit `LENGTH xs = LENGTH ys` assumption *in scope as a term*, even when it's trivially derivable. Same for `EL_MAP` needing `n < LENGTH xs`. Add the precondition as a `by fs []` assumption immediately before the rewrite, or the rewrite is a no-op.
- `metis_tac` can hang for minutes. Cap at ~30s, then `hol_interrupt`. A goal closed by metis stumbling onto the answer = tech debt.

## `[simp]` tags — what tagging actually does
One decision ("should this be `[simp]`?") with four measured consequences. Read all four before tagging a definition.

- **`[simp]` on a DEFINITION removes it as an ATOM from the assumptions.** Every occurrence unfolds, and if the body is an equation `gvs` may then eliminate it outright (measured: assumption `myR a b` present untagged, GONE once tagged) — so every lemma taking that constant as a HYPOTHESIS silently stops matching, and `drule`/`drule_all` fails on a lemma that is plainly applicable. Check what depends on the constant as a hypothesis before tagging.
- **A `[simp]`-tagged definition causes fold↔unfold MISMATCH between an assumption and the goal.** A fact stated with the folded constant `f x` won't match a goal/asm where `simp`/`rw` already unfolded `f` to its RHS (the tag fires there, not in your hand-written term) → `qpat_x_assum`/`simp[folded_thm]` no-ops. Fix: state the bridging fact in the SAME form the surrounding `simp` produces (usually the unfolded RHS). (Distinct from the atom-removal above, which is about the constant disappearing rather than about two forms of it.)
- **Naming a `[simp]`-tagged lemma in a rewrite list is placebo.** `fs[L]` / `gvs[L]` already load every `[simp]` lemma; naming `L` in the list does nothing unless its literal pattern is in the goal. The unblocking step is usually surfacing the literal (via `IF_CASES_TAC`, `Cases_on`, or a rename), not adding the lemma.
- **Tag only what normalizes without looping** — and prefer tagging a PROJECTION helper over the definition itself. A record def `foo s … = <| f1 := X; f2 := Y; … |>` rewriting via simp is O(asms × fields); on a 30+ asm goal it times out at 60s+. Package projections as a `[simp]`-tagged helper (`foo_simps : ∀args. (foo args).f1 = X args ∧ (foo args).f2 = … ∧ …`), proved by `rw[foo_def]`. Now `simp[]` uses the projections without re-deriving the unfold. Same fix when `gvs[record_def]` / `fs[record_def]` times out.

## Case splits, subgoal counts, and `impl_tac`
- `every_case_tac` explodes on big goals — use targeted `Cases_on + gvs`.
- `TOP_CASE_TAC` is variable-name independent — use when `Cases_on` fails due to renaming.
- `Cases_on` branch ordering varies across HOL4 versions / branches. Peek with `hol_state_at`; add/drop `reverse` to flip.
- `strip_tac` does NOT split `A ∨ B`. Use `disj1_tac` / `disj2_tac` to commit.
- `impl_tac` on `(P ⇒ Q) ⇒ G`: `>-` order is `P` first, then `Q ⇒ G` — opposite of the printed display. Trust the live goal list (`hol_goals`).
- `impl_tac >- simp[]` over-reach: if `simp` also closes the bridge subgoal, THEN1 errors with "goal completely solved by first tactic". Narrow with a tight rewrite list.
- **`CONJ_TAC` / `THEN1` "first subgoal not solved" in a discharged region = the positional closing's goal-count shifted, not a broken leaf tactic** (RULE F — count goals mechanically). Two recurring count-shifters: (a) a surviving `if`-guard — a rewrite that discharges a guard *introduced by* a def-unfold must be IN the SAME `simp[…]` call as the unfold (`simp` runs to fixpoint); a separate earlier `simp[EVAL ``g``]` is a no-op because `g` isn't in the goal yet, and the leftover `if g` adds/mangles a conjunct; (b) trivial `x = x` conjuncts from an unfolded abbreviated post-step state. Fix: normalise BEFORE the `conj_tac`/`rpt strip_tac THEN1 …` closing (finish the unfold; `fs[Abbr s]` to collapse `x=x`), or split a disjunction one-goal-per-selector.

## Matchers won't match — `irule` / `match_mp_tac` / `drule`
First check the two silent causes that live elsewhere: a free tyvar in the lemma's own statement (§Polymorphic constructors) and a `[simp]`-tagged constant that is no longer an atom in the assumptions (§`[simp]` tags).

- An out-of-scope variable name silently becomes free, ∀-quantified — `irule` then creates an unexpected existential. Fix: `qexists_tac`. Prevent: name distinct from constants.
- Type-variable mismatch on the conclusion's free tyvars vs goal's concrete types blocks `MATCH_MP_TAC`. `irule` (HO matching) is more lenient.
- `MATCH_MP_TAC` rejects matches requiring type-instantiating a free type variable in the conclusion against a concrete goal type. `match_term` succeeds, `MATCH_MP_TAC` says "No match". Workarounds: `irule` (HO matching, more lenient), `INST_TYPE` upstream, or specialise the theorem statement.
- **`match_mp_tac` (and `irule`) can fail to higher-order match a `fromAList (toAList x)` (or analogous record-wrapped) shape in the goal against a bare bound variable in the theorem conclusion**, even though `Term.match_term` succeeds on the goal/conclusion pair. The tactic's matcher does extra unification through the wrapper that doesn't fire. Workaround: write a typed wrapper theorem with the wrapped pattern baked into the conclusion, then apply that.
- **`qspecl_then [args] mp_tac thm` raises `boolSyntax.dest_forall: not a "!"` when `thm`'s free variables aren't auto-generalised** (stored without explicit outer `∀`). Wrap with `GEN_ALL thm` to lift them, then specialise. Affects `Theorem name:` blocks where the body uses free metavariables.
- **`irule`/`MATCH_MP_TAC` strip the THEOREM's leading `∀`/`⇒` but match the stripped conclusion against the WHOLE goal as-is.** So on a goal `∀x. P x ⇒ C x` and lemma `… ⇒ ∀x. P x ⇒ C x`, `irule lemma` strips the lemma to bare `C x` and fails to match the still-`∀`-quantified goal ("No match"). Fix: `match_mp_tac lemma` matches the FULL conclusion (incl. leading `∀`/`⇒`). When the lemma must instantiate a higher-order var (goal `(∀a b. … f a … f b …) ⇔ (∀j. … f j …)` with `f := λj. g (h j)`), use `ho_match_mp_tac`.
- **Lemma whose hyps mention vars absent from the conclusion (`… ⇒ ∀j. R Xs qs wi j` where the hyps also bind `Ps offset name w wb`)**: `irule`/`match_mp_tac` cannot determine those vars from the goal — `irule` says "No match", `match_mp_tac` leaves them as fresh free vars (often type-clashing). Don't extract such a helper to *apply backward*; either inline its body, or give the conclusion a form that pins every hyp var.
- **Pair-pattern lets unfold to λ-pair redexes that block `drule`.** Unfolding a definition containing `let (a,b) = f x in …` leaves asms shaped `(λ(a,b). body) (f x) = rhs` (an UNCURRY redex); `drule`/`qpat_x_assum`/wrapper-lemma matching on the inner equation `f x = (a,b)` fails until the redex is reduced. Fix: `pairarg_tac \\ gvs []` (introduces `f x = (a,b)` and beta-reduces) or `Cases_on ‘f x’ \\ gvs []`.
- **Apply a conditional-iff lemma (`P … ⇒ (LHS ⇔ RHS)`) with `simp[thm]`, NOT `drule thm`.** simp's side-solver discharges `P` from the asms; `drule` leaves a universal iff whose HO LHS silently fails to rewrite — goal unchanged, next `by` fails unexpectedly.
- Picking an IH after rewrites that scramble assumption order: `first_x_assum` / `last_x_assum` are unreliable; match on the conclusion shape with `qpat_x_assum \`<conclusion-pattern>\` (qspecl_then [...] mp_tac)`.
- **An IH that gained LEADING quantifiers pinned by `(a,b) = f(…)`**: positional `qspecl_then [<orig args>]` mis-binds (1st arg → `a`). Supply the pinned values first (`qspecl_then [‘FST (f …)’,‘SND (f …)’, <orig args>]` + `fs[]`), or `Cases_on ‘f …’` first so `fs` specialises them (also discharges a new `FST e ≠ <bad>` premise).

## Prover-generated names are UNSTABLE — name them stably at the case split
Names like `n0`, `n''`, `v9`, `v15`, `q`, `r`, `x`, `s'`/`s''` introduced by `Cases_on`, `rveq`, `pairarg_tac`, `Cases_on (evaluate _)`, `rw[]`, `strip_tac`, `>~` gvarify, etc. are picked by HOL4's free-name generator. They depend on what's already in scope and can shift across HOL4 versions, library changes, or even adjacent edits in the same script.

- ⛔ **AVOID** depending on a prover-generated name in `Cases_on ‘n0’`, `qpat_x_assum ‘… v15 …’`, `>~ [‘Continue n0’]`, `qspecl_then [‘v15’, ‘r’]`, etc. The next session may rename it and your tactic silently misses (gvarify trap from `>~`) or hard-fails.
- ✅ **Primary tool: `namedCases_on ‘tm’ ["c1_names", "c2_names", ...]`** — performs the case-split AND names each constructor's introduced variables. Empty string `""` for nullary constructors; space-separated for multi-arg. Example: `namedCases_on ‘l’ ["", "head tail"]` splits a list and names the cons args `head` and `tail`. Use this AS the case-split — no rename step needed afterwards.
- ✅ When the split happens via `Cases_on` (no naming control) or `>~` gvarify, recover stably with `rename1 ‘pat’` (single-pass, fail-loud) or `qmatch_goalsub_rename_tac ‘pat’` (subterm). Verify the state (`hol_state_at` / `top_goals()`) after — `rename1` matches the goal's CURRENT shape; if a prior tactic reshuffled, your pattern may not fire.
- ✅ **Existentials: `disch_then (qx_choosel_then [‘a’,‘b’] strip_assume_tac)`** names the witnesses AS THEY ARE INTRODUCED. `strip_tac` on a `∃`-conclusion leaves prover-chosen primed names (`extra'`, `s1'`) that later tactics then pin — same defect as an unnamed case split, different source.
- ⛔ **`rename1 ‘C v’` with polymorphic `C` is type-inference fragile.** When the constructor is polymorphic (`SOME`, `INL/INR`, pair constructors), the free `v` is parsed with a fresh tyvar and HOL infers its type from the FIRST matching `C _` subterm — which may live in an IH, asm, or case-tree where `_` has a different result/option type than the case you intended to rename. Symptom: `Cases_on v` later produces the constructor count of the WRONG type (e.g. `wordSem$result` instead of `loopSem$result`), and a positional dispatcher silently mislabels every arm. Diagnose by `type_of v` immediately after the rename. Fix: use `namedCases_on` AT the split (no rename step), or anchor with a type ascription `rename1 ‘C (v:τ)’`.
- ✅ Prefer **wildcards** `_` for everything not load-bearing for matching: `>~ [‘Continue _’]` is robust; `>~ [‘Continue n0’]` is brittle. Same for `qpat_x_assum` patterns — keep them minimal.
- ✅ When dispatching after a tuple-case (`Cases_on ‘evaluate _’`), reach for the equation by SHAPE not by the auto-name: `qpat_x_assum ‘evaluate (body, _) = (_, _)’ mp_tac` beats `qpat_x_assum ‘_ = (q, r)’`.
- ⛔ **Porting/grafting a tactic chain into a new context re-rolls every generated name it references** (merge repair, sibling adaptation, moving an arm under a different preamble): a `Cases_on ‘… x'.refs’` that worked in the old context silently targets the wrong variable — or errors — in the new one. At the graft entry, insert a `rename1`/`qmatch_*_rename_tac` binding a stable name and rewrite the grafted references to it; never carry the inherited generated name and hope it reappears.
- **`rw []` on `∀x y. ...` clashes with outer-scope `x`/`y`** — HOL renames to `x'`/`y'`, downstream `Cases_on ‘... x ...’` silently picks the OLD `x`. Use `qx_genl_tac [‘k’,‘v’] \\ strip_tac` for fresh, known names.
- **`PairCases_on ‘x’` after a prior `Cases_on ‘x’`** primes new components to `x0'`, `x1'`, ... (not the bare `x0`, `x1`, ...). Match `qpat_x_assum` / `>~` patterns accordingly; don't write tactics from memory of names.

This is a RULE (apply when relevant), not a Gate. Existing scripts often have legacy name-dependencies; touch them only when restructuring is already in scope.

## Polymorphic constructors — annotate at the call site
Bare polymorphic literals (`NONE`, `[]`, `LN`, untyped pair components) parse with fresh tyvars in each occurrence. A pattern's tyvar that doesn't match the goal's tyvar makes `fs`/`gvs` no-op the substitution, `qpat_x_assum` report "no such asm" while `match_term` succeeds, `Cases_on x` claim `x` isn't free.

Better still, when the unanchored tyvar comes from YOUR OWN lemma's statement, pin it IN THE STATEMENT rather than at every call site: a plain implication about a polymorphic result (`f x = Rerr (Rabort Rtype_error) ⇒ …`) leaves the raise type free, whereas stating the same fact as `case f x of Rval … | Rerr …` pins it through the constructor and the lemma then connects everywhere. Otherwise annotate at the call site: `Cases_on ‘(NONE:'a result option, s)’`, `qpat_x_assum ‘… (NONE:'a result option, _) = _’`. Or anchor via a typed neighbour (`sb:('a,'b)state` constrains a sibling `NONE` through the function's signature).

⛔ **`qspecl_then` multiplies this per quotation**: when several spec quotations each embed a type-unanchored subterm (e.g. an all-num-arg constructor inside `f (C x y z)` spec'd into a tyvar-free binder), EACH occurrence-group gets its OWN fresh tyvar — one instantiated theorem can carry `:α`, `:β` AND `:γ` copies of the same syntactic term; its premises then never match the asms while PRINTING identically (`aconv` false — diagnose with a first-diff subterm walk, never the display). Fixes: ascribe EVERY embedded occurrence (`‘… (C x y z : 'a ty) …’` — an OUTER ascription on the quotation does NOT reach occurrences nested inside other applications; the literal tyvar name does pin to the goal's), or include a type-bearing goal variable in the quotation, or sidestep qspecl: pin through a typed asm with `qpat_x_assum ‘<typed asm>’ (mp_then (Pos hd) mp_tac thm) \\ disch_then (drule_at (Pos last))`.

## `>~` / `>>~-` gvarify trap
Goes through `kRENAME_TAC`, which `gvarify`s the goal+asms first (every free var → genvar) before matching. Source: `~/research/HOL/src/q/Q.sml:681`.

- Pattern free vars are treated as **fresh binders**, even when the name already exists in the goal. After unrename, the matched term is renamed TO the pattern's name — context is silently rewritten and you may lose information you needed.
- `(* g *)` / `(* sa *)` annotations restrict the selector — they do NOT disable gvarify.
- For literal-name matching that preserves context, call `Q.MATCH_RENAME_TAC pat` (whole-term) or `Q.MATCH_GOALSUB_RENAME_TAC pat` (subterm) directly — these bypass gvarify. Tactics, so first goal only; for list-tactic form use `THEN_LT FIRST_LT (Q.MATCH_RENAME_TAC pat) THEN1 body`.
- Use `>~` only when pattern's bound names truly are fresh, OR are wildcards `_`. Otherwise expect rename pollution.

### `>>~- ([pat], body)` semantic — body must fully close every matched goal
⛔ **`>>~- ([pat], tac)` runs `tac` on ALL goals matching `pat`; `>~ [pat] >- (tac)` runs it on the FIRST match only.** They coincide only when exactly one goal matches — check that before rewriting either form into the other.

Parses to `LSelectThen(Rename(pat), body >> First[])` (`~/research/HOL/src/parse/TacticParse.sml:192`). `First[]` always fails, so body must produce zero residual subgoals across ALL matching goals. When body leaves residue on even one matching goal, `>>~-` fails opaquely with `[‘pat’]` as the failing-tactic display — no inner step counter, no goal text.
- **Decomposition diagnostic.** Split `>>~- ([pat], body) → >~ [pat_1] >- body  >~ [pat_2] >- body  ...` with per-goal distinguishers. Each `>~` consumes ONE goal, localizing which one breaks the body. Then sub-suspend that single arm.
- **Distinguishers come from asm literals, not from semantic intent.** Patterns must match what the goal/asms LITERALLY contain at the dispatch point. Verify BEFORE writing the chain: `length (List.filter (fn (asl,g) => exists (can (find_term (can (match_term pat)))) (g::asl)) (top_goals ()))` per pattern. If any returns 0, the pattern doesn't distinguish what you think (classic miss: keying on a subterm literal that is IDENTICAL across the arms while the real discriminator sits elsewhere in the asms).

### Discriminate by state-shape, not body-variable name
A pattern like `>~ [\`f x y\`]` where the variables `x`/`y` reuse the body's actual variable names will gvarify and match BOTH a case where the body uses `x`/`y` AND a case where it uses other names — after unrename, the second case's vars are renamed, so the Resume's display lies about which arm it is. To partition semantically distinct case-split branches, key off a unique fragment of state — a specific function call, a specific assumption shape — that only one branch produces. Use `_` wildcards for everything not load-bearing for discrimination.

### `>>` catch-all does NOT rename
A trailing `>>` after `>~ [pat] >- ...` arms is a list-tactic catch-all that does NOT rebind. Bodies reached via `>>` see ORIGINAL variable names; bodies reached via `>~ [pat]` see PATTERN names. Either give every arm a `>~` rename so all bodies share a vocabulary, or write the catch-all body with the goal's actual names.

### Bare-constructor overload trap
`>~ [\`FFI\`]`-style patterns where the bare name is a constructor shared by multiple datatypes (different arities/types) hit HOL's parse-time overload resolution. The resolved constant may have a type that doesn't match the target goal; `kRENAME_TAC` fails silently — surfaces as `Tactical.FIRST_LT: No goal on which tactic succeeds`. Verify with `show_types := true; print (term_to_string \`\`NAME\`\`)`.
- Prefer **qualified** patterns: `>~ [\`Module$FFI _ _ _ _ _ _\`]` with explicit wildcards for arity.
- Or pick a unique local var from the constructor's signature; safe only when the name appears literally in the goal.
- Constructors declared in only one datatype are safe with bare names.

## Arithmetic — `decide_tac`, `ARITH_TAC`, nat subtraction
- **`decide_tac` / `DECIDE_TAC` use arithmetic assumptions.** They undisch the assumptions classified as arithmetic before running `ARITH_TAC`; non-arithmetic assumptions stay out of the decision problem. If irrelevant arithmetic asms make the tactic slow or false, discard them with `qpat_x_assum ‘…’ kall_tac` / `pop_assum (K all_tac)`, or move only the facts you want into a clean subgoal.
- **Natural-number subtraction truncates.** `n - m = 0` when `m ≥ n` on `:num`. `simp` does NOT specialise subtraction by hypothesis-derived order; either case-split on `m ≤ n` first, or hand the goal to `decide_tac` (which handles truncation directly).
- Nat assoc `A + B - 1` vs `A + (B - 1)` does NOT auto-unify under `simp/fs`. Pre-normalise: `\`A + B - 1 = A + (B - 1)\` by decide_tac \\ pop_assum SUBST1_TAC`.
- **`intLib.ARITH_TAC` "Uneliminable natural number term remains"** = the goal and the relevant assumptions are in DIFFERENT numeric types (nat goal `qs❲j❳ < LENGTH Xs` but the facts you need are `int`: `&qs❲j❳ = v − offset`, `v ≤ offset + &LENGTH Xs − 1`). Fixes: (a) `mp_tac` ONLY the needed int (in)equalities then `rpt (pop_assum kall_tac)` so ARITH sees a clean self-contained single-type problem; (b) bridge with an explicit int lemma instead — `integerTheory.INT_LT` (`&m<&n ⇔ m<n`), `INT_OF_NUM` (`&Num i = i ⇔ 0≤i`, an IFF so use as a `simp` rewrite not `irule`), `INT_SUB_LE` (`0 ≤ x−y ⇔ y≤x`). `simp[GSYM integerTheory.INT_LT]` can LOOP — avoid.
- **Nonlinear terms (variable products) anywhere in scope — goal or asms — can hang `intLib.ARITH_TAC`** even when the needed reasoning is linear. The linear decider can treat a nonlinear subterm only as an opaque atom: first derive every fact you need ABOUT that atom as a hypothesis (via dedicated lemmas, e.g. `integerTheory.INT_MUL_SIGN_CASES`/`INT_LE_MUL`), then `kall_tac` the hypotheses that constrain it nonlinearly, and let ARITH finish on the linear residue.

## Assumption context — blowups, consumption, scoping
- **`fs`/`gvs`/`metis_tac` blow up to 60s when the assumption context holds many large `∀…`/`EVERY (… (abstr (…)) )` hypotheses** (common after decomposing a big encoder's constraint blocks). A clean-context copy of the same tactic runs in <0.5s. Fixes: `qpat_x_assum ‘<pat>’ kall_tac` the loop-prone block/∀ hyps you no longer need BEFORE the asm-scanning step (e.g. drop `EVERY _ _` and stale `∀`-equations once their relational facts are extracted); or extract a `[local]` helper stating exactly the needed hyps and prove it in isolation (mind the hyp-only-var application trap in §Matchers).
- **`first_x_assum irule >> simp[]` backtracks expensively across every `∀`-assumption** (and `irule` cannot match a CONJUNCTION goal at all), turning a one-liner into a multi-minute search. Target the intended assumption by shape: `qpat_x_assum ‘<distinctive pattern>’ (qspecl_then [...] mp_tac) >> fs[]`.
- `qpat_x_assum` / `first_x_assum` / `dxrule` consume the matched asm. Use the non-x variants when needed again.
- **Subgoal scoping — `‘P’ by tac` / `subgoal` / `sg`: asm mutations DO NOT escape.** A subproof runs on `(parent_asms, P)`; its asm rewrites are discarded — the parent gets `P` added to UNMODIFIED asms (subproof = call-by-value; identical for all three forms, swapping them changes nothing). Symptom: a chain closed interactively (unfolds accumulated at top level) fails on file replay with `decide_tac`/`ARITH_TAC` "Tried to prove generalised goal … but it was false" — RULE G, not a tool bug. Fix: hoist any unfold/asm-rewrite the parent's closer needs into the MAIN chain, never inside a subproof.
- **`‘Q’ by fs [def]` may not close when `def` has leading `∃`.** fs unfolds the existential in the asm but may leave the witness unpopped under `by`'s strict-close mode, so `Q` doesn't match an asm directly. Use a derived corollary `def_IMP` that names the field/consequence (`state_rel s t ⇒ t.clock = s.clock`), then `imp_res_tac def_IMP`.

## Definitions and their downstream ripple
- A predicate from `Overload p = ‘‘λx. …’’` has no `p_def` rewrite: `simp`/`gvs` won't case-split `p x` or fire a guarded hyp `p x ⇒ Q`. Establish it explicitly (`‘p x’ by … \\ res_tac`/`fs[]`) or unfold the λ. (A `Definition` `p`'s `_def` triggers both.)
- **Definitional rename ripple.** When `foo` → `foos` (singular→list, or any analogous rename), every `simp/fs [foo_def]` in the consumer chain becomes a no-op on the new `foos`-built term — must update to `simp [foos_def, …]`. Not just the binder shapes; the def-unfolding rewrites too.
- ⚠️ **Editing a `Definition` regenerates its auto-`<f>_ind`**, so EVERY downstream proof using `recInduct <f>_ind` re-derives against the new induction theorem and can break in cases UNRELATED to your edit (shifted prover-gen names, reshaped case-tree, an absorbed/added guard). After a semantics tweak expect mechanical fallout across all `recInduct <f>_ind` proofs, not just the case you touched. Proofs inducting on a *different* function's `_ind` are unaffected — that's the cheap immunity check when triaging what rebuilt-broke.

## TRY anti-patterns (the hol4-proving skill bans them; spotter's guide)
- `TRY (chain ... NO_TAC) >> fallback` — fallback runs on every surviving subgoal whether or not it should.
- `TRY rename1` / `TRY Cases_on` for branch detection — silent skip. Use `qmatch_goalsub_abbrev_tac` (fails loudly) or `>~ [pat] >-` shape dispatch.
- `TRY (... NO_TAC)` to skip non-matching subgoals — handle each case explicitly.

Naive in-place TRY substitutes also fail; only suspend/Resume works. See [[feedback_suspend_resume]].

## hol_send hygiene and proofManager
- `e tac` runs on the FIRST goal only — every step, including a chain built by repeated `e(...)`, which must still be assembled as one tactic before persisting. `proofManagerLib.elt lt` runs a list_tactic. To test whether one tactic closes EVERY open subgoal, put `\\ tac` in the FILE and replay — `eall`/`ALLGOALS` on a goalfrag parked by `hol_state_at` is a banned unguarded all-goals driver ([[feedback_replay_discipline]] §goalfrag).
- ⛔ **Raw `e`/proofManager output prints the goal stack TOP-LAST** — the block just above the trailing `: proof` is the CURRENT goal; the first printed block is the deepest pending one. Reading the first block as "the top goal" mis-targets the next `e` (and any `sg`/`by` you aim at it). Use `hol_goals` (goal 1 = top) to read order.
- Interactive `e`/`elt` calls accumulate proofManager state. To keep probes independent, `proofManagerLib.drop()` between unrelated tests.
- ⛔ **Probe the REAL goal, never a hand-reconstruction.** `set_goal`/`prove`/`g` of a typed-out copy of a live subgoal drops the real asm context → false pass on a goal the proof never presents (RULE G), then breaks in file-form (`set_goal` only for a genuinely self-contained lemma). Navigate to the live subgoal with `hol_state_at` and probe THAT. Full rule + sub-suspend fallback: [[feedback_replay_discipline]] §Navigate in an ACCURATE state.
- **Name shadowing:** HOL4's flat top-level value namespace makes `val gs = top_goals();` permanently shadow `gs`. Never bind names that are tactic combinators (`gs`, `fs`, `rw`, `simp`, `e`, `b`, `g`, `it`) or HolKernel functions (`concl`, `hyp`, `dest_thm`, `tag`). In doubt, fully-qualify (`Thm.concl`). The server REJECTS `val <one of these> = ...` in `hol_send` mechanically — a BLOCKED message means rename (`val my_gs = ...`), not retry.
- **Diagnose a hanging/looping tactic by capturing the goal, not re-running the proof.** `val G = proofManagerLib.top_goal()`, then time each candidate as `(tac G; "ok") handle Interrupt => "hang"` under a tight `hol_send` timeout — isolates the tactic from replay/file confounds and bisects the chain in seconds.
- **A previously-fast theorem that suddenly hangs after heavy interactive probing** (cause unconfirmed; possibly an accumulated proofManager goal stack): first check whether the HOL process is actually spinning (a stray tactic loop pegging CPU) vs a bloated idle goal stack; cheap fix `proofManagerLib.drop()` / `dropAll()` (clears the goal stack, keeps theory + heaps loaded). ⛔ `hol_stop`+`hol_start` is an ABSOLUTE LAST RESORT — it reloads the whole ancestor `.dat` chain (minutes on a big file). Prevention: one focused `e`/`sg`, then `drop()`. If it happens, PROMPT THE USER before recovering, so it can be diagnosed properly.

## `hol_check_proof` semantics
- Processes the entire proof as one `e(...)`. `>>` applies to ALL subgoals.
- If a `Cases_on >> gvs[]` chain doesn't solve a branch, subsequent `by`/`>-` blocks run on multiple goals and fail.
- Trace `n→m`: goal count BEFORE→AFTER the entire stack, not just the focused subgoal.
- Returns `CHEAT (not verified)` for `cheat`-containing proofs and shows no goals — use it only for end-of-theorem on cheat-free proofs.
- ⛔ Auto-cheats prefix theorems that error or exceed the per-theorem timeout, so a green `Status: OK` can mask a broken/slow dependency. Full mechanism + the `⚠ depends on cheat` marker: [[feedback_replay_discipline]] §auto-cheat.

## Reading a `>-` / `THEN1` / `\\`-chain arm's goal → SUB-SUSPEND
`hol_state_at` can't navigate inside a parenthesized `>-`/`THEN1`/`\\`-chain group that is applied to several goals (returns the lumped step ENTRY, `target INSIDE step k`); a group receiving one goal it enters on its own (`[inside opaque step k …]`). Sub-suspend the arm to read/develop/diagnose it — the default, ~99% of the time. Canonical rule (incl. cheat-frontier-FORWARD vs sub-suspend-for-FAILURE): hol4-proving skill, HOL4 — suspend/Resume + RULE I. Navigation-limit recovery: [[feedback_replay_discipline]] §state_at navigation limit.

**Extract a `[local]` lemma INSTEAD only when the parent's heavy asm context is ITSELF the problem** (gvs/metis blowups): `Resume` replays the full parent prefix so it does NOT shrink that context; a fresh lemma stating exactly the needed facts does. When extracting, mirror the needed hyps (hyp-only vars become `∃` under `irule` — fill with `qexists`/`irule_at Any`), then apply Gate 6 (single-use nav-helpers inline back).

## Proof strategy
- **Search before guessing tactics — and before PROVING any lemma or DEFINING any concept**, even one a plan lists as to-prove: `hol_search` the statement first; an ancestor may already export the lemma (re-proving an ancestor's `[simp]` lemma is pure duplication) or define the constant (a theory's `.sig` lists its exports for name-browsing). Use `hol_search(query=, pattern=)` (DB.find/DB.match without `hol_send`) — returns matched theorems. Cheap, use freely. Common prefixes for numeric / list / option reasoning: `ADD_`, `MULT_`, `LE_`, `LT_`, `EVEN_`, `LENGTH_`, `MAP_`, `MEM_`, `LIST_REL_`, `OPTION_`. Goals with `<=>` or `=` typically need a rewrite lemma; goals with `∀ … ⇒` typically need a `match_mp_tac` / `irule` lemma.
- **`rpt strip_tac` is a fine opening for `∀x. P ⇒ Q`.** Strips quantifiers and antecedents in one go, surfacing the actual obligation. Use `gen_tac` / `qx_gen_tac ‘k’` when you want named binders without absorbing the antecedent yet.
- **Cheap automation as a probe, not a commit.** Try `simp[]`, `decide_tac`, `EVAL_TAC`, `metis_tac[]` with a short cap (~5–10s) before reasoning by hand — they often dispatch trivial residual goals. But never SHIP a chain whose closer took >5s; that's an accidental proof, not a discharge (hol4-proving skill — No accidental proofs). When several closers work, ship the simplest/cheapest (`simp`/`gvs` beats `metis_tac` beats anything slower).
- **Cheat helper lemmas first, prove later.** Write the helper STATEMENT, close with `cheat`, use at call-site, discharge after the main theorem closes — helper signatures are usually under-determined.
- **New/central definition → prove its interface lemmas first** (projections, intro/elim, characterizing equations); prefer them over raw `simp[foo_def]` unfolds downstream. What to tag `[simp]`: §`[simp]` tags. Related instances: the projection helper there, and extract-one-clause in §Rewriting that loops.
- After `match_mp_tac` / `irule` on a multi-conjunct precondition, lead with `simp[]` or `gvs[]` — most subgoals close as direct asm copies. Only `>-` the residual conjuncts.
- For suspend/Resume sequencing across constructors, see [[feedback_suspend_resume]].

## Recovery
- Smart-quote errors (`unknown character "\226"`): `python3 ~/hol4-mcp/check_quotes.py <file> --fix`. Don't hand-fix with sed/xxd.
- `holmake` target = Theory name; `fooProofTheory` from `fooProofScript.sml`. Use `jobs` param or `HOL4_MCP_HOLMAKE_JOBS` for parallel builds.
- ⛔ **`PROOF BROKEN` / `replayed=0/N` / wrong-looking goal / "No such label" → the FIRST diagnosis is ALWAYS a YOU problem** (tactic that fails in file-form; subgoal attacked after a reordered/incomplete prefix; a line *inside* a `\\`-chain that lands at the chain ENTRY; an unparenthesised `by`-chain; a dispatcher that never reaches its QED; a wrong/auto-generated identifier). "Cache/desync" is essentially never the cause; don't whitespace-bump/backward-nav/restart. PROVE it: replay file-form (`hol_state_at` past QED, or `holmake`) — it fails there too. Full diagnostic: [[feedback_replay_discipline]] §desync; "No such label" specifically: [[feedback_suspend_resume]].
